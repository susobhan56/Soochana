How to use Soochana

Soochana turns official figures on Odisha's people into stories, maps and charts, for the state and each of its thirty districts. This manual shows what the portal is for, who it serves, and where to find every page, tool and figure.

Chapter 1

What Soochana is

The name

Information, made known

Soochana (ସୂଚନା) is Odia for "information; the act of making known". The portal's promise is in its tagline: from numbers to narrative.

It is run by the Demographic Intelligence Unit (DIU) of CYSD, with technical support from UNFPA Odisha, and is part of the Odisha Development Initiative.

What it is for

Four ways in

  • Dashboards to see the state and every district at a glance.
  • Stories that explain what the numbers mean, in plain words.
  • A repository of datasets and reports to download.
  • Policy communication: figures ready for planning, briefs and meetings.

Where the numbers come from

125 years of official records

The Census of India from 1901 to 2011; the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-4, NFHS-5 and NFHS-6, 2023–24); the Sample Registration System, PLFS, UDISE+ and LASI; and official projections to 2036.

Every figure names its source and year beside it. Figures after 2011 are projections, and are marked as such.

Chapter 2

Finding your way

The menu bar

The logo always brings you home

Every page has the same menu bar at the top. Click the Soochana logo from anywhere to return to the home page. The Part of ODI logo beside it opens the Odisha Development Initiative's website in a new tab.

The three main links

About Us, Themes, Repository

  • About Us: what Soochana is, its partners, team and common questions.
  • Themes: six topics: Demography, Health, Education, Ageing, Migration and Urbanization, Employment.
  • Repository: datasets, reports and sources to view or download.

Open About Us Open Themes Open the Repository

The Discover menu

Four more ways to explore

Open Discover for Learn (short lessons), Tectonics (illustrated stories), Planning (what the outlook means for each district) and the Guided tour.

Language

English or Odia

On the home page, the EN / ଓଡ଼ିଆ switch changes the headings, buttons and section names. The portal remembers your choice next time.

The Report menu

Present it or print it

On the home page, Report offers Present, a full-screen mode you move through with the arrow keys, and Download report, the whole portal in one printable document.

Two helpers on most pages

The Assistant and the bell

The round button at the bottom right opens the Soochana Assistant: ask a question in everyday words. The bell lists what is new on the portal in the last 30 days.

Chapter 3

The home page

Your first visit

A live count to welcome you

The first screen shows a live estimate of Odisha's population. Choose Enter Soochana for the home page, or Take the guided tour for an eight-scene introduction.

The count moves smoothly between the yearly projections. It is an estimate, not a head count.

Sections 1 and 2

The headline and the story

The Introduction sets the scene, with two buttons: Read the story and Find your district.

The story, "How one crore became five", tells Odisha's population as 100 dots in six chapters. Use the chapter tabs to jump between them.

The dots on the right edge of the home page are a map of its sections. Click one to jump there.

Sections 3 to 5

What changed, and by topic

  • What changed: NFHS-5 (2019–21) beside NFHS-6 (2023–24).
  • A generation of gains: survival, care and living standards over four decades.
  • By topic: pick a tab for its key figures, each with a source.

Sections 6 and 7

Where the districts are heading

District direction shows, for six measures, how many of the 30 districts improved between the two surveys. Hover a dot for a district's figures.

The window to 2036 explains the demographic dividend years and the road to 2047.

Chapter 7

Help along the way

Soochana Assistant

Ask in your own words

It answers from the portal's own data and names the source and year. It knows all 30 districts (Balasore or Baleswar, Keonjhar or Kendujhar), about 40 indicators, and groups such as KBK or the coastal districts.

Follow up with "and Puri?" or "and the lowest?". It remembers what you were asking about.

Good to know

Small tips

  • Seeing an old page? Press Ctrl + F5 (Cmd + Shift + R on a Mac).
  • On a phone, every page works; charts sit above the text.
  • Sharing a view? Planning keeps your district in the page address, so the link opens the same view.
  • More questions? See Questions at the bottom of the home page, or About Us.
