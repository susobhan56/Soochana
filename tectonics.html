Soochana Tectonics

The ground beneath everything is moving

Demography moves like tectonic plates: slowly, out of sight, and then all at once. Schools, hospitals, jobs and marriages all sit on top of it.

Here two plates meet. One is the official record: Census, NFHS, SRS. The other is the signals people leave online: what they search for, ask and buy. Where the two collide, new questions rise.

    How many of Odisha's women have been online?

    In NFHS-5 (2019–21), 25 of every 100 women aged 15–49 had ever used the internet. How many do you think it was in NFHS-6 (2023–24)?

    25 of 100

    52 of 100

    The share roughly doubled, from 24.9% to 51.8%, in about four years. Men moved too, from 50.7% to 73.2%. In towns it is 66.7% of women, in villages 49.0%.

      And how many used modern contraception?

      Modern methods are the pill, condoms, the IUD, injectables and sterilisation. In NFHS-5, 49 of every 100 currently married women used one. As women went online, did that rise or fall?

      49 of 100

      41 of 100

      Use of a modern method fell 8 points, from 48.8% to 40.8%. Use of any method fell from 74.1% to 66.2%. And the women who want to wait before their next child but use nothing, the unmet need for spacing, rose from 2.6% to 4.7%.

      Two lines, pulling apart

      In the same four years, women came online in large numbers, fewer couples used modern contraception, and more women wanted to wait for a child with nothing to help them wait.

        Story 1 · Chapter 4 · The new land

        Three questions rising from the fault

        These are questions, not findings. Each says what would prove it and what would rule it out, so the data can answer back.

        1. Open question

          The chemist's counter

          Are more couples relying on pills bought over the counter, such as emergency pills? Use that comes and goes is hard to capture in a question about the method a woman uses now.

          Holds if searches for emergency pills rise faster than searches for other methods.

          Falls if they stay flat while modern method use keeps falling.

        2. Open question

          Fear travels faster

          Are worries about side effects spreading online faster than counselling can reach people?

          Holds if searches about side effects rise, and outnumber searches about where to get a method.

          Falls if side-effect searches stay small or flat.

        3. Open question

          Waiting, without a way to wait

          Unmet need for spacing nearly doubled. Is the wish to delay a child growing faster than the services built for it?

          Holds if searches about delaying a first pregnancy, or the gap between children, rise.

          Falls if they stay flat. Then the change is happening offline, and the silence is the finding.

        Story 1 · Chapter 5 · The aftershock

        If the ground moves, what shakes?

        If the questions hold, the change reaches far beyond one survey table.

        • Health

          Counselling may need to go where the questions are asked: on phones, in Odia, and in private.

        • Young people

          18.6% of women aged 20–24 married before 18. For many, the questions start before marriage, and the answers need to as well.

        • Chemists

          The counter may already be a front line of family planning, and a source of data no survey reaches.

        • Planning

          A survey every four years cannot keep pace with change this fast. Signals can tell planners where to look in between.
