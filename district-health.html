Odisha, four years on: faster access, heavier bodies

A comparison of the National Family Health Survey rounds NFHS-5 (2019–21) and NFHS-6 (2023–24) across 102 indicators for Odisha and its 30 districts. Of the indicators where a direction is clearly better or worse, here is how the state moved.

Every indicator, one dot

Each dot is a state indicator, placed by how many points it moved in the healthy direction. Hover for details; select a dot to open it in the district explorer.

The story in pictures

Imagine 100 people (or 100 households) from Odisha. Each figure below is one of them. Pick a topic to see how many of those 100 were affected five years ago and how many today.

Blue figures show something good (like insurance). Orange figures show a problem (like stunting), so for orange, fewer is better.

What the data says, in plain words

Seven shifts that matter most. Each card shows the numbers, what happened, and what it probably means. Grey bar is NFHS-5, coloured bar is NFHS-6.

Correlations (r) are across the 30 districts in NFHS-6. They show which things move together, not what causes what; women’s schooling, for example, also stands in for household wealth and urban living.

Source: NFHS-6 (2023–24) and NFHS-5 (2019–21) district and state fact sheets for Odisha, as supplied. Values in parentheses in the source (based on 25–49 unweighted cases) are marked † and shown faded; values marked * (fewer than 25 cases) and NA are omitted. Indicator 20 (third-or-higher births) and total fertility are not reported at every level. Region groups follow Odisha’s three revenue divisions; region and KBK figures are unweighted averages of district values. Direction labels (better/worse) are analytical judgements: rising overweight, blood sugar and blood pressure are treated as worse; caesarean rates, fertility, sterilisation, traditional methods and public/private facility shares are treated as having no clear direction.
