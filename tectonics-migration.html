Soochana Tectonics · Story 5

Leaving home

Every season, people leave Odisha's villages for brick kilns, building sites and factories far away. Many come back. Some leave again.

Migration is the plate that moves fastest, and the one the official record sees least clearly.

      Story 5 · Chapter 1 · The plate

      Who is moving into Odisha?

      In 2007–08, of every 100 people who had moved into Odisha from another state, 24 were Odias coming home. How many do you think it was in 2020–21?

      24 of 100

      67 of 100

      Up from 24.3% to 66.5%. Into villages it is 83.4%. Odisha has turned from a state people leave into one more people enter, and most of those entering are its own people coming back.

      2020–21 includes the return during COVID-19. Before 2020, DIU estimates the share at 60.1%, still the highest in India.

      Official · NSS 2007–08 and PLFS 2020–21, DIU estimates

      Story 5 · Chapter 1 · The plate

      How many leave for the season?

      The last official count of short-term migrants, in 2007–08, found about 4.8 lakh across the whole state: 1.2% of its people. Then a field survey visited 99,523 rural households in coastal and western Odisha.

      How many households had someone away for the season?

      30.8%, nearly one household in three. Scaled up, that is about 15.3 lakh seasonal migrants in those two regions alone, three times the official count for the whole state.

      The two use different methods and years, so they cannot be matched exactly. But a gap this wide says the official record sees only part of the movement.

      Official · NSS 2007–08

      Field survey · CMLS and Aajeevika Bureau, 2014

      Story 5 · Chapter 1 · The plate

      From outflow to inflow

      Odisha's net migration rate turned from −1.6% to +0.74%: more people now arrive than leave. The share of Odias living in another state fell, from 2.9% to 2.2%.

      Those who still leave are changing. From the towns, 40.3% have a degree, up from 25.9%. Most men who leave now hold salaried jobs. The poorest still leave too, but for a season, and the record barely sees them.

      Official · NSS 2007–08 and PLFS 2020–21, DIU estimates

        Story 5 · Chapter 1 · The plate

        Where is there no town to go to?

        People leave when there is nowhere nearby to go. In 2011, 16.7% of Odisha lived in towns, against 31.1% for India. Which district do you think is the least urban?

        Boudh, where 4.6% live in towns. Then Nuapada (5.6%) and Kendrapara (5.8%). Khordha is the most urban, at 48.2%.

        Now ask whether towns are growing. In four districts, Boudh, Debagarh, Koraput and Nuapada, towns grew more slowly than villages from 2001 to 2011. DIU's notes single out Boudh and Nuapada as among the districts most prone to people leaving.

        By 2036 Odisha is projected to be 21.5% urban, against 39.6% for India.

        Official · Census 2001 and 2011

        What do people search before they leave?

        A departure starts with a question: where is the work, who is the contractor, which train. We are tracing four groups of searches made in Odisha, in Odia and in English, from 2019 to today.

        Each trace is search interest from 0 to 100, relative to its own busiest week. Watch for a yearly rhythm: departures after the harvest would show as peaks at the same time each year.

          Story 5 · Chapter 4 · The new land

          Three questions rising from the fault

          These are questions, not findings. Each says what would prove it and what would rule it out.

          1. Open question

            The calendar of leaving

            Do people leave on a calendar, after the harvest each year, in numbers no survey has counted since 2007–08?

            Holds if searches for distant jobs, contractors and trains peak at the same time every year.

            Falls if the traces show no yearly rhythm.

          2. Open question

            Home, but for how long?

            Two in three people moving into Odisha from other states are Odias coming back. Is it a homecoming, or a pause before leaving again?

            Holds if searches for jobs inside Odisha rise and stay up.

            Falls if searches for jobs far away climb back within a year or two.

          3. Open question

            Villages left to the old

            Story 3 found villages older than towns. Is that the young leaving?

            Holds if the districts that lose the most people each season have the oldest households.

            Falls if there is no link between leaving and ageing across districts.

          Story 5 · Chapter 5 · The aftershock

          When people move, what moves with them?

          Migration carries money home, takes workers away, and leaves gaps in families and villages.

          • Counting

            Seasonal leaving has had no official count since 2007–08. A state that cannot count its movers cannot plan for them.

          • Towns

            Where towns are not growing, people go further. Small towns that can absorb work nearby may keep families together.

          • Families

            Children and the old stay behind. Schools, health workers and pensions in the villages carry the load.

          • Return

            Two in three arrivals are Odias coming home. Work and skills for those who return decide whether they stay.
