Soochana Tectonics · Story 2

When does youth want to marry?

Marriage sets the clock for much of what follows: how long a girl stays in school, whether she works, when her first child comes.

In Odisha that clock is moving. But it moves slowly, it moves differently in each district, and in some places it is moving the wrong way.

      How many young women married before 18?

      Eighteen is the legal age for a woman to marry. In NFHS-5 (2019–21), 21 of every 100 women aged 20–24 had married before it. How many do you think it was in NFHS-6 (2023–24)?

        And young men, before 21?

        For men the legal age is 21. In NFHS-5, 13 of every 100 men aged 25–29 had married before it. Did that change?

        13 of 100

        13 of 100

        Almost no change: 13.3% then, 12.5% now. And a surprise: in towns it is 15.1%, higher than in villages (12.1%). For women it is the other way round.

          Where do girls marry youngest?

          The state is not one plate. It is thirty. Which district do you think has the highest share of women aged 20–24 who married before 18?

          Rayagada (34.0%) and Malkangiri (33.1%) lead, both in the south. Third is Angul, 30.2%: an industrial district, and it rose 5 points.

          18 districts fell and 12 rose. Koraput fell 14.6 points, Nabarangapur 12.3, Nayagarh 10.7. Bargarh rose 6.3.

          Each district figure rests on a small sample, so a move of a few points can be chance. The arrows mark only moves of 5 points or more, and even those need the next survey round to confirm them.

          Fast plates, slow plates

          Some things moved fast. Women online doubled. Women with ten or more years of school rose 11 points.

          Marriage before 18 fell by only 2 points, and for men before 21 it barely moved. School and phones are changing young lives quickly. The age at marriage is following slowly.

            Story 2 · Chapter 4 · The new land

            Three questions rising from the fault

            These are questions, not findings. Each says what would prove it and what would rule it out.

            1. Open question

              School first, marriage next?

              Women's schooling rose 11 points; marriage before 18 fell 2. Is school delaying marriage, or do girls marry soon after they leave?

              Holds if the districts where schooling rose most are also where early marriage fell most. The NFHS district tables can already test this.

              Falls if the two move independently across districts.

            2. Open question

              Where the plates split

              Koraput fell 14.6 points while Bargarh rose 6.3. What did the falling districts do differently?

              Holds if the falling districts share something: programmes, schooling gains, or more searches about the legal age.

              Falls if the moves vanish in the next round. Then they were sampling noise.

            3. Open question

              Choice before age

              Are more young people choosing their own partner, even while the age at marriage barely moves? No survey asks.

              Holds if searches for love and court marriage rise faster than searches for matrimony sites.

              Falls if they stay flat.

            Story 2 · Chapter 5 · The aftershock

            If the clock moves, what moves with it?

            Marriage age is upstream of almost everything else in a young woman's life.

            • Schools

              If school delays marriage, keeping girls in school past class 10 is a family-planning policy as much as an education one.

            • Health

              6.5% of girls aged 15–19 are already mothers or pregnant: 7.2% in villages, 2.8% in towns. Early marriage still sets the clock for early motherhood.

            • Districts

              A state figure of 18.6% hides districts above 30%. Plans need to follow the map, not the average.

            • Young men

              Men in towns marry before 21 more often than men in villages. Who they are, and why, is a question no one has asked yet.
