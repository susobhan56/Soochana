# Soochana — Presenter Script

A spoken walkthrough of the portal, module by module. Read the **Say** lines
aloud (in your own words is fine), follow the **Do** lines on screen, and use
the **Next** line to move to the following module.

- Full walkthrough: about 30–35 minutes. A 10-minute version is at the end.
- Open the site before you start and close the welcome screen once, so you
  control when it appears. To show the welcome screen again, open a new
  private window.
- Every number below is the one the site shows. If a number on screen differs,
  trust the screen.

---

## 0. Opening (1 minute)

**Do:** Have the home page (`index.html`) open, welcome screen showing.

**Say:**
> "Soochana is an Odia word. It means *information*, the act of making
> something known. This is Odisha's Demographic Intelligence Portal, built by
> the Demographic Intelligence Unit at CYSD with technical support from UNFPA
> Odisha, and part of the Odisha Development Initiative.
>
> Our line is *from numbers to narrative*. Odisha has a lot of official data:
> the Census, the National Family Health Survey, the Sample Registration
> System, labour and education surveys. Most of it sits in PDFs and tables
> that only specialists open. Soochana takes that record and turns it into
> something a planner, a student, a journalist or any citizen can read in a
> few minutes.
>
> One thing to say up front: we describe how the population is changing. We
> don't argue for more people or fewer people. We support voluntary,
> rights-based choices."

## 3. Home — "Odisha, told in 100 people" (5–6 minutes)

**Say (intro):**
> "This is a five-minute story in six chapters. The trick is simple: we
> shrink Odisha to 100 people. The picture stays still and the dots
> rearrange as you scroll. They're the same 100 dots all the way down."

**Chapter 1 · Who**

**Say:**
> "About 4.63 crore people live in Odisha, so each dot stands for about 4.6
> lakh people.
>
> 83 of the 100 live in a village; only 17 in a town or city. Khordha, home
> to Bhubaneswar, is the most urban district at 48%. Boudh is the least, at 5%.
>
> 23 of the 100 belong to a Scheduled Tribe, from 62 communities, 13 of them
> particularly vulnerable tribal groups. Across India the share is about 9
> in 100. Odisha has about 3% of India's people but 9.2% of its tribal
> population."

**Chapter 2 · How we got here**

**Say:**
> "Now the dots become a line: 125 years of population. In 1901 Odisha held
> about 1.03 crore people. For fifty years it barely grew; between 1911 and
> 1921 it actually shrank.
>
> Then came the boom. As fewer people died young, the population grew 2.9
> times in sixty years, to 4.2 crore in 2011. Projections take it to 5.15
> crore by 2036.
>
> But look at the dashed end of the line: it's starting to bend. Growth
> peaked at 2.5% a year in the 1960s. By 2031–36 it's projected at about half
> a percent. Between 2011 and 2021 Odisha grew 8.9%, against 12.6% for India."

**Chapter 3 · Why it is slowing**

**Say:**
> "Now each dot is a district. 2.1 children per woman is the replacement
> level, where each generation just replaces itself. In 2020 only 5 of 30
> districts were above it. Nabarangpur was highest at 2.61, Jharsuguda lowest
> at 1.32. The state as a whole is at 1.8.
>
> By 2036, 28 districts are projected below replacement. Only Nabarangpur and
> Rayagada stay above. Families are getting smaller everywhere; just at
> different speeds."

**Chapter 4 · What it does**

**Say:**
> "Back to our 100 people. In 2021: 23 children under 15, 66 of working age,
> 11 aged 60 or over. With so many working-age adults, Odisha is inside its
> *demographic window*: more earners than dependents.
>
> By 2036: 21 children, 63 working age, 16 elders. In real numbers, people
> over 60 go from 51 lakh to 82 lakh, up 60% in fifteen years. The window
> won't stay open forever."

**Chapter 5 · Where**

**Say:**
> "Averages hide very different places. Scheduled Tribes are the majority in
> Mayurbhanj, Malkangiri and Rayagada, and almost absent in Puri.
>
> Literacy runs from 86.9% in Khordha to 46.4% in Nabarangpur: a 41-point
> gap inside one state.
>
> Ageing follows the same line. By 2036 Jagatsinghapur is projected to be the
> oldest district and Malkangiri the youngest. The coast grows old first; the
> tribal south stays young for longer.
>
> And you can click any district on this map to open its full profile."

**Chapter 6 · What it asks**

**Say:**
> "The last card asks the real question: what does this mean for schools,
> jobs and hospitals? And it links to a printable report of everything on
> this page, which I'll show you later."

**Next:** Scroll to **Odisha in numbers**.

## 14. Tectonics — the story series (4–5 minutes)

**Do:** Click **Tectonics** in the header. Use the arrow keys or the rail to
move between screens.

**Say (the idea):**
> "Tectonics is our experiment. Every story puts two plates side by side.
>
> The blue plate is *the record*: official surveys like the Census and NFHS.
> They're reliable but slow; they arrive every few years.
>
> The amber plate is *the signals*: what people in Odisha search for online,
> from Google Trends. Fast and noisy, but it can hint at change before the
> survey catches it.
>
> Where the two plates meet, we get new questions."

**Say (how a story works):**
> "Each story runs the same way. First, *you guess* the official number.
> Then we reveal it. Then the search traces. Then *you predict* which way
> searches moved against the survey. The result can be a *confirmed shift*,
> an *early tremor* where searches move before the survey, a *hidden change*
> the internet missed, or a *fault line* where they disagree.
>
> Then open questions, each with what would prove it right or wrong. And
> every number carries a badge saying how sure we are of it: official,
> administrative, search signal, people's voices, or projection."

**Do:** On story 1, do the first guess with the audience.

**Say:**
> "Story 1: *Online, but unprotected?* What share of women in Odisha have
> ever used the internet? Guess." *(Reveal: from about 25% to about 52%
> between the two surveys.)* "And modern contraceptive use? It went the other
> way, from 48.8% down to 40.8%. More women online; fewer using modern
> methods. That's the question the story chases."

**Say (the season, one line each):**
> "There are five stories so far:
> 1. *Online, but unprotected?* Internet use and contraception.
> 2. *When does youth want to marry?* Child marriage, district by district,
>    and how it tracks girls' schooling.
> 3. *The ageing aftershock.* The 60-plus population, with an animated
>    pyramid: when will elders outnumber children under 10? Around 2036.
> 4. *Covered, but connected?* Health cover jumped from about 48% to 83% of
>    households, but high blood sugar in men rose too.
> 5. *Leaving home.* Migration: two-thirds of people moving into Odisha are
>    now Odias coming home, and a field survey found far more seasonal
>    migrants than the official count."

**⚠ Presenter note:** The search-trace screens currently show **"No reading
yet"**: the Google Trends files haven't been added. If you reach that screen,
say:
> "The search data is being collected now with the DIU team's list of the
> actual Odia and English words people use. We'd rather show an empty panel
> than a made-up line."

## 16. Soochana Assistant — the chatbot (2 minutes)

**Do:** Click the Assistant button (bottom corner). Type questions live.

**Say:**
> "On every page there's the Soochana Assistant. Ask it a question in plain
> English."

**Try these (tested; ask them in this order, as the second and third follow on
from the first):**
- "What is the infant mortality rate in Koraput?" *(34.6 per 1,000, down
  from 42.9)*
- "And Puri?" *(22.7: it remembers you were asking about infant mortality)*
- "And the lowest?" *(Cuttack, 16)*
- "Which district has the highest child marriage?" *(Nabarangpur, 39.4%)*
- "Compare KBK with coastal districts on stunting" *(39.2% against 22.7%)*
- "What should Nabarangpur focus on?" *(its strengths and gaps)*
- "Population of Ganjam in 2036" *(41.22 lakh, the largest district)*

**Say:**
> "It understands all 30 districts, even with different spellings like
> Balasore or Keonjhar, and about 40 indicators by their everyday names. It
> can rank, compare, show trends and point you to related reports.
>
> Two important things. Every answer names its source and year. And it isn't
> a general AI chatbot: it answers only from the portal's own data, inside
> your browser. It won't make things up."

## Likely questions — short answers

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Where does the data come from? | Census of India 1901–2011, NFHS-4, 5 and 6, SRS, PLFS, UDISE+, ORGI projections, DIU's own Bayesian projections to 2036, and Government of Odisha reports such as the Economic Survey 2025-26. Each figure names its source on screen. |
| How are 2021 and later figures made, with no 2021 Census? | They're projections. DIU uses a Bayesian method, alongside the official ORGI projections. They're marked as projections wherever they appear. |
| How often is it updated? | Whenever a new survey round or report comes out. The bell shows what changed. NFHS-6 has already been added. |
| Can I use the charts and data? | Yes. Charts download as images, datasets download as Excel from the Repository, and the report saves as PDF. |
| Does the chatbot use ChatGPT or send my questions anywhere? | No. It runs entirely in your browser and answers only from the portal's own data. |
| Why use Google searches in Tectonics? | Surveys come every few years; searches show what people are worried about now. We treat them as early signals, never as proof, and label them that way. |
| Does it work on a phone? | Yes, the pages are built to work on phones. |
| Is it free? | Yes, it's open to everyone, no login. |

## 10-minute version

If time is short, show only these, with the shorter lines:

1. **Opening** (30 sec): what Soochana means and who built it.
