Life Expectancy

Odisha's life expectancy of 70.4 years is an average built from this year's death rates, not a forecast for any one baby.

By DIU Team · Data: SRS abridged life tables 2020–24, district estimates from NFHS-4 and NFHS-5
The idea in one picture

Life expectancy is the average number of years 100 babies would live if today's death rates never changed.

When we say "life expectancy in Odisha is 70.4 years", it is easy to hear "people in Odisha die at 70". That is not what it means. This page builds the number step by step, so you can see what it does say.

By sex, the figure is 69.2 years for men and 71.8 years for women (SRS abridged life tables, 2020–24).

Follow 100 babies

Start with 100 imaginary babies. Each year, take away the share who would die at that age, using this year's death rates.

A few die as babies. Then very few die through childhood and youth. Deaths pick up again after about 50. Drag the slider to move through the ages.

Why the area is the average

Look at the curve one year at a time. If 90 of the 100 babies are alive at age 40, they live 90 years of life between them in that year.

Add this up over every age and you get the shaded area. It is the total years lived by all 100 babies. Divide by 100 and you have the average:

years lived by
all 100 babies ÷ 100babies = life expectancy at birth

Press Same area as a block. The same area is squeezed into a rectangle. Its width is 70.4 years. That is what "average" means here: if every baby lived exactly the same length of life, it would be 70.4 years.

Why babies matter so much

The infant mortality rate (IMR) is the number of babies, out of 1,000 born, who die before their first birthday. Odisha's IMR is 28 (SRS).

A baby who dies loses a whole life. In our illustration, that is about years taken out of the total. A death at age 70 takes away about years. So a few baby deaths pull the average down a lot.

Try other infant death levels. Everything else stays the same.

This is why life expectancy rose so fast across India as fewer babies died. Saving a baby adds a whole lifetime to the total. Saving the life of an old person adds a few years.

It also explains a common puzzle. When life expectancy was low, it was not because everyone died young. Many babies died, and that dragged the average down.

Districts: women live longer

In every district of Odisha, women are expected to live longer than men. This is true almost everywhere in the world. It is partly biology and partly that men face more risks from work, accidents, tobacco and alcohol.

The chart shows estimates for each district. They are built from NFHS survey data, which is a different source from the SRS state figure of 70.4. The two use different methods, so compare districts with each other, not with the state number.

Common mistakes

Myth

"Life expectancy is 70, so most people die at 70."

Fact

It is an average. Deaths are spread over many ages. In our illustration, half of all babies live past . A person who reaches 60 lives, on average, to about .

Myth

"A baby born today will live 70.4 years."

Fact

It uses this year's death rates for every age. If death rates keep falling, as they have for decades, today's babies will probably live longer. It is a snapshot, a period measure.

Myth

"Low life expectancy means few people grow old."

Fact

A low average is often caused by many babies dying. Those who survive childhood can still live long. Try the infant death buttons above.

Remember

  1. Life expectancy at birth is the average years 100 babies would live if today's death rates at every age never changed.
  2. It is the area under the survival curve, divided by the number of babies. It is not the age at which most people die.
  3. Baby deaths pull it down a lot, because each one removes a whole lifetime. Fewer infant deaths raised it fast.
  4. Odisha: 70.4 years (men 69.2, women 71.8; SRS 2020–24). District figures come from NFHS and a different method, so compare them with each other.

Data and method

State figure. Life expectancy at birth of 70.4 years (male 69.2, female 71.8) is from the SRS abridged life tables for 2020–24. IMR of 28 per 1,000 live births is from SRS.

The 100-babies illustration. The survival curve is not Odisha's life table. It is a smooth Gompertz–Makeham curve (death risk = a small constant plus a part that grows about 8.5% with each year of age after age 1), with 2.8% of babies dying in the first year, most of them in the first weeks. The rising part is tuned so that the area under the curve equals 70.4 years. Medians and "average age at death after 60" quoted on this page come from this illustration, not from SRS. Life expectancy is computed as the sum of person-years lived (trapezoids by single year of age, with infant deaths placed early in the first year) divided by the starting number.

Infant deaths. The infant-mortality buttons change only the first-year death risk and keep all later death rates fixed, so the change in life expectancy shows the effect of infant deaths alone. In real life, child and adult death rates have also fallen.

Districts. District life expectancy for men and women (datasets/district_life_expectancy.json) are estimates derived from NFHS-4 and NFHS-5 data. They are a different source and method from SRS, which is why most district values are higher than the SRS state figure. Use them to compare districts and the gap between women and men, not as replacements for the SRS number.
