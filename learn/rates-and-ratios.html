Rates and ratios

A big district always has more births and deaths, so we divide by its size to compare places fairly.

By DIU Team · Data: Census of India, SRS, NFHS-5, DIU projections · Charts are computed live in your browser
The idea in one picture

A rate tells you how often something happens out of every 1,000 people, so places of any size can be compared.

The pictures above are an illustration, not real data.

Rate
How often something happens, out of a fixed number of people.
Ratio
One number compared with another, like women compared with men.
Per 1,000
"Out of every 1,000". 28 per 1,000 is 2.8 out of 100.
IMR
Infant mortality rate: babies who die before age 1, per 1,000 born alive.
Sex ratio
Number of females for every 1,000 males.

Why per 1,000?

Ganjam is Odisha's biggest district by population. Boudh is one of the smallest. If both had the same number of deaths in a year, would they be equally healthy? No.

The same number of deaths is a much bigger share of Boudh's people. To see this, divide the count by the population. Then multiply by 1,000, so the answer reads "out of every 1,000 people".

Why 1,000 and not 100? Deaths and births are rare in a single year. "0.79 out of 100" is hard to say. "7.9 out of 1,000" is easier. For very rare events, like mothers dying in childbirth, we go further and use "per 1,00,000" (per lakh).

Infant mortality: 28 out of 1,000

Odisha's infant mortality rate (IMR) is 28. It means that out of every 1,000 babies born alive, 28 die before their first birthday.

Below, each square is one baby. The orange squares are the 28 who do not reach age 1. Switch the picture to see other rates. Watch what each one is "per": people, or babies born alive.

Two sources, two methods The district bars come from a sample survey (NFHS-5), which asks mothers about their babies born in the past few years. The state line comes from SRS, which records births and deaths as they happen. The methods and years differ, so do not read a bar above the line as an exact comparison. District survey numbers also wobble, as the lesson Where the numbers come from shows.

Sex ratio: females per 1,000 males

The sex ratio is a ratio, not a rate. It compares two groups: the number of females for every 1,000 males. Exactly 1,000 means equal numbers. Below 1,000 means fewer females than males.

Growth rate: how fast are we growing?

The growth rate says how much the population grows in a year, as a percentage. 1% a year means about 1 more person for every 100 people, each year.

You do not need to understand "ln" to use it. Think of it as the tool that spreads growth evenly over the years, allowing for growth on top of growth (like interest in a bank).

Remember

  1. A rate divides a count by the number of people it could happen to. That makes big and small places comparable.
  2. Always check "per what": per 1,000 people, per 1,000 babies born alive, or per 1,00,000 births.
  3. Sex ratio compares females with males (1,000 = equal). Growth rate is the percentage added each year.

Data and method

State rates. Birth rate 15.8 and death rate 7.9 per 1,000 people, and IMR 28 per 1,000 live births: SRS Statistical Report 2024. MMR 124 per 1,00,000 live births: SRS Special Bulletin on Maternal Mortality in India 2022–24. Birth and death rates divide by the mid-year population; IMR and MMR divide by live births in the same period.

District IMR. Estimates based on NFHS-5 (2019–21) as compiled in the portal's mortality file. They come from mothers' birth histories in a sample survey, so they carry sampling error, and they cover a different period from SRS.

Population. Census of India 1901–2011. District populations for 2021 and state figures for 2021–2036 are DIU projections (Bayesian approach). The 1936 row in the state file (a mid-decade estimate) is left out of the charts.

Growth. The bars use the growth figures in the portal's state file, which divide each period's total growth by the number of years (simple average). The calculator uses the exponential rate, r = ln(P₂ ÷ P₁) ÷ T × 100, which allows for compounding, so it gives slightly smaller numbers for the same period. Doubling time is ln 2 ÷ r, about 69.3 ÷ r.
