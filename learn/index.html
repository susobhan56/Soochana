Which numbers move together?

The lessons in Track 2 use one table: indicators for each of the districts. The grid below compares every pair. Each square answers one question: when one number is high in a district, is the other one high too?

Click any square to see the districts behind it

Moving together is not causing Two numbers can rise and fall together without one causing the other. Both may follow a third thing, such as income or remoteness. And with only 30 districts, a weak link (a pale square, around ±0.3) can appear by pure chance.

About Soochana Learn

Track 1 explains the methods behind the figures shown across Soochana: Census counts, NFHS survey estimates, SRS vital rates, and the DIU population and fertility projections (Bayesian approach). Where a picture is an illustration rather than real data, the lesson says so. Track 2 is inspired by MLU-Explain, Amazon's visual machine-learning essays, rebuilt around Odisha's own statistics. Its models are deliberately small and transparent: they teach how prediction works and where it goes wrong, not to rank districts or guide funding. The modelling table is built by learn/tools/build_learn_data.py from files already in the portal. Download the table (JSON).
