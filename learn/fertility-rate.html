Total Fertility Rate (TFR)

Odisha's TFR of 1.8 means a woman would have about 1.8 children in her life if today's birth rates held.

By DIU Team · Data: NFHS-5 (2019–21), DIU fertility projections (Bayesian approach), SRS
The idea in one picture

TFR adds up this year's birth rates at every age, as if one woman lived through them all.

TFR
Average children a woman would have at today's birth rates.
Age-specific fertility rate
Babies born in a year per 1,000 women of one age group.
Replacement level (2.1)
The TFR at which each generation just replaces the one before.
Projection
A careful estimate of future years, if recent trends continue.

You will often read that "Odisha's TFR is 1.8". It sounds like a count of children. It is really a summary of this year's birth rates. This page shows how that one number is built, and what it can and cannot tell you.

One imaginary woman

Women of different ages have babies at different rates. Women aged 20–24 have the most. Very few women over 40 have a baby in any year.

Each bar below is one age group. Its height is the age-specific fertility rate: babies born in one year for every 1,000 women of that age.

Now imagine one woman who lives from 15 to 49 and has babies at exactly these rates. Press the button and follow her.

Why multiply by 5?

Each rate is for one year of life. But each age group covers five years. Our woman spends five years aged 20–24, so she collects that rate five times.

So we add up the seven rates and multiply by 5:

babies per 1,000 women,
seven groups added × 5years in each group ÷ 1,000to get "per woman" = TFR, children per woman

No real woman is followed for 35 years. All the rates come from one year of births. That is why TFR responds quickly when birth patterns change.

Why 2.1?

Every child has two parents. If, on average, each woman has two children who grow up, the next generation is the same size as this one. The population then neither grows nor shrinks in the long run.

The extra 0.1 does two jobs. Some children die before they grow up. And slightly more boys than girls are born, so a few more births are needed to replace each mother. That gives the replacement level of 2.1.

Odisha's TFR of 1.8 is below replacement. Most districts are below it too. But not all. The chart shows each district's TFR in 2020.

Where fertility is heading

DIU has projected every district's TFR up to 2036. A projection is not a promise. It asks: if recent trends continue, where will this number be?

The projections use a Bayesian approach. In plain words, each district's own trend is combined with what happened in places that went through the same change earlier. This keeps the lines sensible even where data are thin.

Pick a district. Its line is in colour. The other 29 districts are the faint grey lines.

TFR is not the birth rate

Odisha's birth rate is 15.8. Its TFR is 1.8. They measure different things.

Birth rate

Babies born in a year for every 1,000 people. Everyone counts: children, men, old people.

So it depends on how many young women live in the place.

TFR

Children per woman, built from the rates at each age.

It does not depend on how many young women there are. That makes it fair for comparing places.

Here are two towns with 100 people each. In both, women have children at exactly the same rates, so both have a TFR of 1.8. Only the age mix differs.

Town A has twice as many young women, so twice as many babies, and twice the birth rate. Yet a woman in either town has the same number of children on average. When you compare districts, TFR tells you about women's choices and chances. The birth rate mixes that up with the age structure.

Remember

  1. TFR is the average number of children per woman, if this year's birth rates at each age stayed the same for her whole life.
  2. It is built by adding the seven age-group rates and multiplying by 5, because each group covers five years.
  3. 2.1 is replacement level. Odisha (1.8) and most districts are below it. A few southern districts are still above.
  4. TFR is per woman. The birth rate is per 1,000 people, so it also depends on how many young women there are.

Data and method

Definition. TFR = 5 × Σ ASFRa, summed over the seven five-year groups 15–19 … 45–49, where ASFRa is births in a year to women in group a divided by the number of women in that group. It is a period measure: it describes one year (or survey window), not the completed family size of any real group of women.

The age pattern. The portal does not hold Odisha's own age-specific rates, so the "imaginary woman" chart uses a typical Indian age shape (peak at 20–24) rescaled so that 5 × the sum equals Odisha's TFR of 1.8 (NFHS-5, 2019–21). Treat the bar heights as an illustration.

Districts. District TFR for 2020 is computed from NFHS-5 district data; 2021–2036 values are DIU projections using a Bayesian approach (datasets/district_fertility_trends.json). The file's NFHS-4 column is not used, because it is known to be wrong. Projections assume recent trends continue and carry uncertainty that grows with time; the lines show central values only.

Birth rate. Odisha's crude birth rate of 15.8 per 1,000 is from the SRS 2024 report. The two-town picture assumes each woman aged 15–49 has the same chance of a birth in every year, which is a simplification used only to show why the age mix matters.
