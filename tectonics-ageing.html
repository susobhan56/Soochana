Soochana Tectonics · Story 3

The ageing aftershock

Fewer children are being born, and people are living longer. The result builds slowly, and then arrives all at once: a state that is growing old.

By 2036 Odisha is projected to have twice as many people aged 60 and over as it had in 2011. Who will care for them, and who will pay?

      How many people in Odisha are 60 or older?

      In NFHS-5 (2019–21), 13 of every 100 people in the households surveyed were 60 or older. How many do you think it was in NFHS-6 (2023–24)?

      13 of 100

      16 of 100

      Up from 13.4% to 15.6% in about four years. And villages are older than towns: 15.9% against 13.9%.

      NFHS counts the people living in the homes it visits. Population projections, which count everyone, put the share lower, at about 12% for 2026. Part of the gap may be young people living away from home.

      When will people aged 60+ outnumber children under 10?

      It happens between 2031 and 2036. In 2031 children under 10 still lead, 73.2 lakh to 70.2 lakh. By 2036 it is 81.9 lakh people aged 60 and over against 69.2 lakh children.

      For every 100 people of working age there will be 25 aged 60 and over, up from 15 in 2011.

      Who grows, who shrinks

      Set every age group to 100 in 2011 and follow it to 2036. Children shrink to 88. The working age grows to 127. People aged 60–79 double.

      The oldest grow fastest of all: 80 and over, from 4.0 lakh to 9.2 lakh. And most of them will be women: 4.9 lakh women against 4.2 lakh men.

        Story 3 · Chapter 1 · The plate

        Where will Odisha grow old first?

        In 2011 no district had more than 12 people aged 60+ in every 100. Which district do you think will be the oldest in 2036?

        Jagatsinghapur (17.9%), Puri and Cuttack (both 17.5%): the coastal heartland.

        Every district gets older. The fastest is Khordha, home to Bhubaneswar: from 9.1% to 17.3%. Next come Sundargarh, Sambalpur, Jharsuguda and Kendujhar, the industrial and mining belt.

        These are projections, not counts. They assume today's trends in births, deaths and migration carry on.

          Story 3 · Chapter 4 · The new land

          Three questions rising from the fault

          These are questions, not findings. Each says what would prove it and what would rule it out.

          1. Open question

            Care becomes a market

            Is looking after parents moving from something families do to something they buy?

            Holds if searches for home nurses and old-age homes rise faster than the number of older people.

            Falls if they stay flat while the old population grows.

          2. Open question

            The boom towns grow old

            Khordha and the industrial belt age fastest. Are the people who came there for work growing old far from the families they left?

            Holds if the districts that drew the most migrants in past decades are the ones ageing fastest now.

            Falls if ageing speed has nothing to do with past migration.

          3. Open question

            Old age is a woman's story

            By 2036 women will outnumber men among people aged 80 and over. Many will be widows. Are pensions and care reaching them?

            Holds if pension records and pension searches lean towards women, and grow with the oldest group.

            Falls if women are under-represented among those claiming support.

          Story 3 · Chapter 5 · The aftershock

          When a state grows old, what shakes?

          Ageing is not only a number in a projection. It arrives in hospitals, in homes and in household budgets.

          • Health

            Health cover now reaches 82.7% of households, up from 47.9% (NFHS). Does it cover what old age needs: long illness, care at home?

          • Families

            When the young move away for work, who looks after the old who stay behind in the villages?

          • Money

            By 2036 there will be 25 people aged 60+ for every 100 of working age, up from 15 in 2011. Pensions and savings will have to stretch further.

          • Towns

            Ramps, buses, clinics and homes built for the young will need rethinking, first along the coast and in the industrial belt.
