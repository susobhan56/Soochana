Soochana Tectonics · Story 3

The ageing aftershock

People in Odisha are living longer, and families are having fewer children. Both are gains. Together they build slowly, and then arrive all at once: a state that is growing older.

By 2036 Odisha is projected to have more than twice as many people aged 60 and over as it had in 2011: 83.5 lakh, from 38.9 lakh. Planning for their care can start now.

      How many people in Odisha are 60 or older?

      In NFHS-5 (2019–21), 13 of every 100 people in the households surveyed were 60 or older. How many do you think it was in NFHS-6 (2023–24)?

      13 of 100

      16 of 100

      15.6% in NFHS-6 (2023–24, published May 2026), from 13.4% in NFHS-5 (2019–21). And villages are older than towns: 15.9% against 13.9%.

      NFHS counts the people living in the homes it visits. The official projections, which count everyone, put the share lower, at 13.0% for 2026. Part of the gap may be young people living away from home.       The pyramid becomes a pillar

      Press play. In 1991 Odisha was a pyramid: many children, few elders. Watch the yellow bars, the people aged 60 and over, as the years pass.

      When will people aged 60+ outnumber children under 10?

      It happens between 2026 and 2031. In 2026 children under 10 still lead, 67.3 lakh to 61.5 lakh. By 2031 it is 72.2 lakh people aged 60 and over against 63.9 lakh children, and by 2036, 83.5 lakh against 60.2 lakh.

      For every 100 people of working age there will be 27 aged 60 and over in 2036, up from 15 in 2011. The median age rises from 30.2 years in 2021 to 36.4 in 2036.

      Story 3 · Chapter 1 · The plate

      Who grows, who shrinks

      Set every age group to 100 in 2011 and follow it to 2036. The working age grows to 122. People aged 60–79 double, to 204. Children ease to 77, as families choose to have fewer.

      The oldest grow fastest of all: 80 and over, from 4.0 lakh to 10.5 lakh, a sign of longer lives. And most of them will be women: 5.9 lakh women against 4.6 lakh men.

        Where will Odisha grow old first?

        In 2011 no district had more than 12 people aged 60+ in every 100. Which district do you think will be the oldest in 2036?

        Jagatsinghapur (21.5%), Kendrapara (21.1%) and Nayagarh (20.6%), with Puri (19.7%) and Cuttack (19.3%) close behind: the coastal heartland.

        Every district gets older. The largest rise is in Jagatsinghapur: from 12.0% to 21.5%. Kendrapara, Nayagarh, Puri and Cuttack follow. The youngest in 2036 are Malkangiri (13.0%), Rayagada (13.5%) and Nabarangapur (13.5%), in the south.

        These are projections, not counts. Source: Census 2011; UNFPA India, District Level Population Projections in Selected States of India, 2021 & 2026 (2022). The 2036 figures are Soochana estimates: the UNFPA 2026 projection carried forward with the ORGI state projection.

          Story 3 · Chapter 4 · The new land

          Three questions rising from the fault

          These are questions, not findings. Each says what would prove it and what would rule it out.

          1. Open question

            Care becomes a market

            Is looking after parents moving from something families do to something they buy?

            Holds if searches for home nurses and old-age homes rise faster than the number of older people.

            Falls if they stay flat while the old population grows.

          2. Open question

            The coast grows old first

            Jagatsinghapur, Kendrapara, Puri and Cuttack are projected to age first. Is that longer lives, fewer births, or young people working elsewhere?

            Holds if the districts that sent the most young workers away in past decades are the ones ageing fastest now.

            Falls if ageing speed has nothing to do with past migration.

          3. Open question

            Old age is a woman's story

            By 2036 women will outnumber men among people aged 80 and over. Many will be widows. Are pensions and care reaching them?

            Holds if pension records and pension searches lean towards women, and grow with the oldest group.

            Falls if women are under-represented among those claiming support.

          Story 3 · Chapter 5 · The aftershock

          When a state grows old, what shakes?

          Ageing is not only a number in a projection. It arrives in hospitals, in homes and in household budgets.

          • Health

            Health cover reaches 82.7% of households in NFHS-6 (2023–24, published May 2026), from 47.9% in NFHS-5 (2019–21). The next step is cover for what old age needs: long illness, care at home.

          • Families

            When the young move away for work, who looks after the old who stay behind in the villages?

          • Money

            By 2036 there will be 27 people aged 60+ for every 100 of working age, up from 15 in 2011 (ORGI Technical Group projections, 2020). Pensions and savings can be planned for that now.

          • Towns

            Ramps, buses, clinics and homes can be made to work for older people too, first along the coast.
