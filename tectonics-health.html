Soochana Tectonics · Story 4

Covered, but connected?

In four years, health cover in Odisha jumped further than anything else the national health survey measures. Most households now hold a card.

But a card is a promise, and the illnesses are changing. Does the cover reach the care people now need?

      Story 4 · Chapter 1 · The plate

      How many households have health cover?

      In NFHS-5 (2019–21), 48 of every 100 households had at least one member covered by a health insurance or financing scheme. How many do you think it was in NFHS-6 (2023–24)?

      48 of 100

      83 of 100

      Up from 47.9% to 82.7%: the biggest jump of all 102 things the survey measures in Odisha. And villages are ahead of towns, 86.0% against 65.2%.

        Meanwhile, how many men have high blood sugar?

        The survey tests blood sugar with a finger prick. In NFHS-5, 17 of every 100 men aged 15 and over had high blood sugar or were taking medicine for it. Four years later?

        17 of 100

        27 of 100

        Up from 17.0% to 26.6% in about four years. For women, 14.0% to 21.7%. In towns it is 30.0% of men.

        Where the care goes

        As cover rose, births by caesarean section rose too, from 21.6% to 29.4%. In private hospitals, 76.8% of births are now by caesarean. The share of births in public facilities fell, from 78.7% to 75.0%.

        And the illnesses are changing: more people overweight, more diabetes. These are illnesses of years, not of a single hospital stay.

        Official · NFHS-5 and NFHS-6, Odisha

          Story 4 · Chapter 1 · The plate

          Where is cover thinnest?

          Every district gained cover. Which one do you think has the least?

          Khordha, home to Bhubaneswar, at 71.9%. Then Jharsuguda, Cuttack, Sundargarh and Sambalpur: the cities and the industrial belt. The best covered are in the south and west, led by Nabarangapur at 89.6%.

          Now switch to blood sugar, and the map flips. In the five least-covered districts, 24.6% of women have high blood sugar; in the five best covered, 16.7%. Across all 30 districts: the more cover, the less diabetes.

          This does not mean cover prevents diabetes. Towns have both less cover and more diabetes, so the link may run through town life, not through the card. District figures rest on small samples.

            Story 4 · Chapter 4 · The new land

            Three questions rising from the fault

            These are questions, not findings. Each says what would prove it and what would rule it out.

            1. Open question

              The card and the private hospital

              Cover jumped while births moved away from public facilities and caesareans rose. Is the card pulling people towards private hospitals?

              Holds if the districts where cover rose most are where public-facility births fell most, and searches for private hospitals rise.

              Falls if the two move independently across districts.

            2. Open question

              Covered for the wrong illness?

              Schemes pay for hospital stays. Diabetes needs years of tests and medicine outside hospital. Is the cover built for yesterday's illnesses?

              Holds if diabetes searches lean towards the cost of tests and medicine.

              Falls if people find what they need within the scheme.

            3. Open question

              The cities left out

              Cover is thinnest in Khordha and the industrial belt, exactly where diabetes is highest. Who are the townspeople without a card?

              Holds if searches about getting a card come mostly from the cities.

              Falls if the uncovered in towns turn out to have private insurance instead.

            Story 4 · Chapter 5 · The aftershock

            If the card is not the cure, what shakes?

            Cover is a door. What matters is where it leads.

            • Primary care

              A finger-prick test in the village costs less than a hospital stay years later. Screening for sugar and blood pressure may matter more than the next card.

            • Hospitals

              If cover pulls births to private hospitals, caesarean rates there, now 76.8% of their births, become a public question.

            • Cities

              Cover is thinnest where diabetes is highest. The urban poor and informal workers may be the next people to reach.

            • Schemes

              Long illnesses need tests and medicine, month after month. Cover that pays only for hospital stays may miss most of the cost.
